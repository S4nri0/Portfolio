Who is Divine?

I am a Chicago-based student passionate about data, sociology, and community building. Outside of my academic work, I dabble in art and writing; I love exploring different mediums, from ceramics and sculpture to sketches and creative writing.

I come from a family of immigrants from the Democratic Republic of Congo 🇨🇩 ! I've lived in the Chicagoland suburbs most of my life.

When I'm not studying or making art, I enjoy exploring the city, trying new coffee shops, and crafting with friends. Here are a few snapshots of my life!

Untitled

My consciousness has been ripped from my flesh,
the transition must have been painless.
I have felt nothing.
My meaty remains orchestrated by butcher twine,
my leather suede shoes clomp toward my chair.
Each day my march becomes more erratic, whatever bones I have,
are liquified.
The spoor of rot follows wherever I go,
I ooze into my chair a final time.
From diseased meat, a heap of maggots, and finally the grot that stains the floor.
Someone has to clean that up.

Poem can be found in the Srping 2025 The Prairie Light Review: Vol. 47: No. 1, Article 37.

Black Willows

"All trees bear fruit, not all fruit is for us,
poisoned by conquest and desire.
Inherit all worldly goods,
accept Divine punishment.
May the branches of willows sweep you away."


The cacophony of the creatures echoed through the wetlands, and a willow tree at the rim of the river swayed back and forth with the wind. Adorned with fireflies dancing between its branches, towering over every other tree, mature yet afflicted. Life flourishes around it, though its roots are rotten; it persists. Thriving not off the land but the words of men, it is not of nature.

The full moon reflected on the stream, making it glow and burst with light.
She is awake tonight.
The wind howls and the sky showers. The earth beneath the tree gave way to her, and she clawed herself out, racing against time. Knowing the soil would swallow her once more if she lingered. Dormant for a lifetime, stuck in decay. For the first time in a long time, Opal was among the living.
